  What I chose, and why

  The card became three zones instead of four stacked rows. A left gutter (color
  bar + rank), a middle identity block (name / POS · TEAM / ADP), and a bye

  long a name gets.

  │▐ │      WR · LAR                │
  │▐ │      ADP 2.7                 │
  └──┴──────────────────────────────┘

  The name shrank from 62 to 40, deliberately. The gutter took real width, and
  something had to give. Your four goals are position, rank-ordering, bye, and
  zone needs — the name is not among them, and it's what you read once you've
  already found the card. 40 is close to the 41 this app originally shipped
  with, and it's sized so "Jaxon Smith-Njigba" exactly fills its 12.5cm column,
  ellipsising anything longer.

  Plate tint at 10%. Enough to separate a wall of RBs from a wall of WRs before
  any glyph resolves; the darkest resulting channel is 0.823 (RB green), leaving
  the 0.08 ink at better than 10:1. Going further starts eating the contrast
  the whole readability pass bought.

  Rank is a soft watermark, not a headline — 56pt at 0.55 alpha in the position
  color, sitting left of the name where it can't fight it. Its rect fits three
  digits, since live data reaches rank 100+. It's assigned in
  DraftDayDataService immediately after the full sort, so it travels on the

  The chip bug — root cause

  Two causes, compounding, both in code I wrote:

  1. RoundedRectangle is opaque by default. Its _blendMode initialises to
  RoundedRectBlendMode.Disabled → BlendMode.Disabled, so the alpha channel of 
  backgroundColor is discarded. BackPlate only gets working alpha because it
  explicitly sets PremultipliedAlphaAuto on its mainPass after initialize() — a
  workaround my standalone chip never inherited. My (0.08, 0.09, 0.12, 0.12)
  "12% wash" rendered as solid near-black.
  2. The label was the same color as the fill. t.textFill.color = ink() is
  (0.08, 0.09, 0.12) — the exact RGB of the chip. The text was drawing,
  invisibly, on top of itself.

  The fix is specified to need no alpha at all: an opaque near-white fill (0.97,
  0.97, 0.99), dark ink text at size 30, and a 0.2cm mid-grey border. It
  renders identically whether or not the blend mode is ever changed — that
  property is the actual fix. I kept the chip rather than reverting to plain
  text because the failure was a color/blend mistake, not the chip concept, and
  the blend-mode trap is now documented in a warning comment so the next
  standalone rect doesn't hit it.
